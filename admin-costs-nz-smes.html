The real cost of admin work in a small NZ business

When we ask a new client how much time admin costs their team, the first answer is almost always an underestimate. Not because they're wrong about the hours, but because the hours are only part of it.

The visible cost: hours

This is the easy part to measure. Someone spends twenty minutes chasing an overdue invoice. Someone else spends half a day a week reconciling bookings across two systems that don't talk to each other. Add it up across a small team and it's often the equivalent of a part-time role, just spread thinly across everyone.

The hidden cost: attention

Admin work is rarely done in one uninterrupted block. It's a form here, a follow-up email there, a spreadsheet update between calls. Every switch back to it has a cost that doesn't show up on a timesheet: the few minutes it takes to remember where you were on the actual work that matters. For a small team, that adds up to a lot of half-finished thinking.

The compounding cost: what doesn't get done

The real cost isn't the admin itself. It's what a business doesn't do because someone was tied up doing it. Follow-ups that happen a day late. A quote that goes out after the customer has already gone with someone else. A new hire's first week spent shadowing someone instead of getting proper training, because there wasn't time to write it down.

Why this is worth fixing before you hire

The instinct when admin piles up is to hire someone to handle it. That can be the right call. But it's worth checking first how much of that admin is actually decision-making, versus how much is just moving information from one place to another. The second kind is usually the cheaper and faster thing to fix, and it's exactly what a well-built workflow or agent is good at.
