AI agents vs chatbots: what's actually different

"AI agent" gets used loosely enough now that it's worth being precise about it, especially if you're deciding what to invest in for your business.

A chatbot answers. An agent acts.

A chatbot is built to hold a conversation and respond with information. Ask it a question, it looks up or generates an answer. That's genuinely useful for things like answering common customer questions, but the conversation is the end of the interaction. Nothing changes in your systems because of it.

An agent is built to complete a task. It can read information from your systems, make a decision based on rules or context, and then take an action: update a record, send a confirmation, flag something for a person to check, book a slot in a calendar. The conversation, if there is one, is just the interface. The point is the outcome.

Why that difference matters for a small business

A chatbot on your website can reduce how many times someone has to email you to ask your opening hours. That's worth having. But it won't reduce the actual admin load on your team, because it isn't doing any of the admin. It's just answering questions about it.

An agent, on the other hand, can be the thing that actually books the appointment, updates the job in your system, and sends the confirmation, without anyone on your team touching it. That's where the hours-back-in-the-week comes from.

Where a hybrid setup makes sense

In practice, most of what we build for clients sits between the two: a conversational front end, so it feels natural for a customer or a staff member to use, connected to an agent underneath that can actually do something with what it hears. The conversation isn't the product. It's just the easiest way for a person to hand off a task.
